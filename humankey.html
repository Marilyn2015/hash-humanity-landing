    HUMANKEY PROTOCOL

    Prove you are human.
    Not who you are.

    HumanKey is the proof-of-personhood system behind Hash Humanity. It combines liveness detection, biometric uniqueness checks, privacy-preserving account controls, and passkeys to support one simple rule: one human, one account.

    WHAT HUMANKEY DOES

    We need to know that you are a person. We do not need your public identity.

    Most websites prove that someone controls an email address, phone number, password, or device. That does not prove that every account belongs to a different human being.

    THE RULE
    1:1
    ONE HUMAN ONE ACCOUNT

    Another email should not mean another identity.

    Creating another email address is easy. Creating another username is easy. Software can automate both. HumanKey is designed so that creating another account requires another unique human being, not simply another credential.

    HOW IT WORKS

    From person to HumanKey.

    HumanKey separates proof of personhood from everyday authentication. The biometric process establishes human uniqueness during enrollment. After enrollment, the account can use a passkey for normal authentication.

    01

    Consent

    Biometric processing begins only after the user is shown the HumanKey process and provides consent.

    02

    Liveness

    The user completes a liveness session. The purpose is to determine whether the camera is seeing a live participant rather than simply accepting a photograph or basic replay.

    03

    Face representation

    A mathematical facial representation is created or obtained for biometric comparison.

    04

    Uniqueness search

    The new enrollment is checked against previously enrolled biometric records to determine whether the same person appears to have already enrolled.

    05

    HumanKey established

    When the required checks pass, the account satisfies HumanKey's proof-of-personhood requirements.

    06

    Passkey created

    The user creates a passkey for everyday authentication. HumanKey does not need to perform a new facial uniqueness scan every time the person signs in.

    07

    Enter Hash Humanity

    The account enters the network with a HumanKey-backed personhood state.

    BIOMETRICS

    Why HumanKey uses a face.

    A biometric characteristic is tied to a physical person in a way that an email address, username, password, or IP address is not. HumanKey uses facial biometrics for a narrow purpose: establishing liveness and helping determine whether the same person has already enrolled.

    Liveness is not face recognition

    Liveness asks whether a real person appears to be physically present during the verification session. Face comparison asks whether biometric representations are sufficiently similar.

    Biometric matching is probabilistic

    Facial comparison does not produce mathematical certainty. Biometric systems work with similarity and confidence measurements.

    The face is not the profile

    Passing HumanKey does not require the social profile to display the person's face. The biometric process exists to support personhood and uniqueness checks.

    The purpose is uniqueness

    HumanKey is not designed to determine a person's legal identity from their face. The purpose is to establish liveness and help detect repeat enrollment.

    BIOMETRIC DATA

    The system needs enough information to recognize the same human again.

    Duplicate-person detection requires a biometric reference that can be compared against a later enrollment.

    The biometric reference exists for a specific security purpose: uniqueness.

    HumanKey does not need a person's face every time they read a post, write a comment, send a message, or sign in. Normal authentication can use the passkey after enrollment.

    PASSKEYS

    Your face establishes personhood. Your passkey gets you back in.

    HumanKey separates enrollment from everyday authentication.

    Passkeys use public-key credentials rather than relying on a reusable password being sent to the server.

    The biometric system answers the personhood question. The passkey handles routine account authentication.

    ACCOUNT RECOVERY

    Losing a device should not mean losing yourself.

    Recovery is separate from creating a new personhood record. When recovery requires biometric verification, HumanKey can use the enrolled biometric relationship as part of establishing that the returning person is the same human who originally enrolled.

    Recovery does not create another human.

    A successful recovery restores access to the existing human account rather than creating a second identity.

    WHAT HUMANKEY IS DEFENDING AGAINST

    The problem is not just bots. It is cheap identity.

    Automated account creation Software can generate usernames, emails, and registration attempts at machine speed. Requiring a live, unique person introduces a physical constraint.
    Duplicate enrollment A different email address does not automatically make the same person a new human.
    Photo and replay attempts Liveness detection helps distinguish a live participant from basic presentation attacks.
    Disposable identities Requiring another unique human makes repeated replacement of accounts more difficult than simply generating another credential.
    Credential theft Passkeys reduce dependence on reusable passwords and use public-key authentication.
    Manufactured consensus HumanKey cannot stop real people from coordinating. It is designed to constrain one operator cheaply presenting itself as a large crowd of independent humans.
    WHAT HUMANKEY DOES NOT DO

    Being human does not automatically make someone right, honest, or decent.

    HumanKey is proof of personhood, not proof of character.

    HumanKey does not determine whether a statement is true or decide what someone is allowed to believe.

    Its purpose is specific. It is designed to make it harder for one operator to manufacture an unlimited number of apparently independent people.

    Hash Humanity is not trying to build a network where everyone agrees. It is trying to build one where the people disagreeing are actually people.
