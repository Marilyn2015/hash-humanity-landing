    The mission behind Hash Humanity

    The internet has
    an identity problem.

    Online, an account looks like a person. But an account is not proof of a person. One operator can control dozens, thousands, or potentially millions of identities. Software can speak, react, follow, post, amplify, impersonate, and manufacture activity at a speed no human population can match.

    Hash Humanity starts with a different question: who is actually human?

    THE CORE IDEA

    The internet does not need more accounts.
    It needs accountable humans.

    Hash Humanity is built around proof of personhood. Before an account becomes another voice in the network, HumanKey is designed to establish that a real, live, unique person is standing behind it.

    THE PROBLEM

    The internet cannot reliably tell a crowd from a machine.

    01 / ACCOUNT CREATION

    Creating another identity is cheap.

    On conventional platforms, creating another account can require little more than another email address, phone number, automated registration workflow, or compromised credential. When identities are disposable, abuse becomes inexpensive to repeat.

    02 / SCALE

    Automation changes the size of the problem.

    A human has limited time and attention. Software does not operate under the same constraint. Automated systems can create posts, replies, follows, reactions, messages, and account activity continuously and at large scale.

    03 / FALSE CONSENSUS

    Ten thousand accounts can look like ten thousand people.

    Account counts are often interpreted as social evidence. Likes, follows, replies, reposts, and repeated talking points can create the appearance of popularity or consensus even when a smaller number of operators are coordinating the activity behind them.

    04 / DISPOSABILITY

    A ban means less when another account costs nothing.

    When an abusive identity can immediately be replaced, moderation becomes an endless game of account removal. The platform removes an account while the underlying operator simply returns under another one.

    05 / TRUST

    Eventually people stop knowing what is real.

    When humans cannot reliably distinguish genuine people from automated identities, coordinated accounts, impersonators, or synthetic activity, trust in the conversation itself begins to erode.

    WHAT AUTOMATION CAN DO

    A bot does not need to be intelligent.
    It needs to be scalable.

    Modern automation, account farms, generative AI, stolen credentials, and coordinated operators can combine to perform activity that once required large numbers of people.

    01

    Manufacture engagement.

    Automated identities can follow accounts, react to posts, repost material, generate comments, and repeatedly amplify the same message, creating activity that appears organic.

    02

    Simulate public opinion.

    Coordinated account networks can repeat narratives, overwhelm replies, create apparent agreement, or make a fringe position appear significantly more popular than it actually is.

    03

    Impersonate people and institutions.

    Fake accounts can copy names, photographs, branding, writing styles, or organizational identities and use those identities for fraud, misinformation, phishing, or social engineering.

    04

    Reach people at machine speed.

    Automated systems can send messages, post content, scrape information, test responses, and target large audiences far faster than a single human operator could manually.

    05

    Adapt content with AI.

    Generative systems can produce large amounts of unique text, images, audio, and other synthetic material, reducing one of the traditional signals that exposed repetitive automated campaigns.

    06

    Return after removal.

    If account creation remains cheap, deleting malicious accounts addresses the symptom rather than the underlying operator. A new identity can simply take the old one's place.

    THE SCALE IS REAL 1.6M bot-driven or fake account signups blocked per hour by Microsoft

    Microsoft reported blocking an average of roughly 1.6 million bot-driven or fake account signup attempts every hour during the period described in its 2025 Digital Defense Report. The important point is not Microsoft itself. It is what the number demonstrates: automated identity creation exists at industrial scale.

    THE HASH HUMANITY MODEL
    1 : 1
    HUMAN ACCOUNT

    One person should not be able to look like ten thousand people.

    HumanKey is designed around one human, one account. That changes the economics of abuse. If every additional account requires another unique human rather than another script, disposable email address, or automated registration, large-scale manipulation becomes dramatically more difficult and expensive to organize.

    HUMANKEY

    Proof of humanity. Not proof of identity.

    HumanKey is Hash Humanity's proof-of-personhood system. Its job is not to learn who you are. Its job is to establish that a real, live person is requesting access and that the same person has not already enrolled another account.

    01

    Consent comes first.

    HumanKey begins only after the user explicitly agrees to the verification process. Camera access and biometric processing are not intended to occur silently in the background.

    02

    Liveness checks for a real person.

    A liveness challenge helps determine whether the camera is seeing a live participant rather than a static image, simple replay, or other basic presentation attack. Liveness does not make spoofing mathematically impossible, but it raises the difficulty substantially compared with ordinary account registration.

    03

    A biometric representation is created.

    The system derives a mathematical facial representation suitable for comparison. The purpose of that representation is duplicate-person detection, not creating a public identity profile.

    04

    HumanKey checks for another enrollment.

    The new representation is compared against existing enrolled representations to determine whether the same person appears to have already created an account. This is the mechanism that supports the one-human, one-account model.

    05

    Personhood becomes a privacy-preserving proof.

    After successful verification, the system can use a derived proof or nullifier-style identifier so the platform can recognize that personhood requirements have been satisfied without requiring a public name, address, government identity, or other unnecessary identifying information.

    06

    A passkey becomes the everyday credential.

    After enrollment, authentication can use a passkey rather than repeatedly scanning the user's face. Passkeys use public-key cryptography and are designed to provide passwordless authentication that is resistant to many forms of credential phishing.

    07

    Biometrics return only when necessary.

    The normal goal is not continuous facial verification. Biometric verification is reserved for enrollment and carefully controlled recovery scenarios where HumanKey needs to re-establish that the returning person is the previously enrolled human.

    WHAT THIS CHANGES

    Human verification does not fix the internet by itself.
    It changes what is possible.

    01
    Bot farms become harder to scale.

    Scripts can create unlimited software processes. They cannot create unlimited unique human beings. Requiring unique personhood places a physical constraint in front of digital account creation.

    02
    Disposable accounts become less disposable.

    If one person cannot simply create another verified identity, losing an account carries more consequence. That can make bans, rate limits, and reputation systems more meaningful.

    03
    Account counts become more meaningful.

    A verified account does not prove that the person is truthful, wise, or acting in good faith. It does provide stronger evidence that there is a distinct person behind that presence.

    04
    Manufactured crowds become more expensive.

    Coordinated groups can still organize. But requiring another verified human for another account makes it much harder for a single operator or automated system to cheaply multiply itself into an artificial crowd.

    05
    Anonymous does not have to mean artificial.

    A person can choose not to reveal their civil identity while still proving that a unique human stands behind the account. Personhood and public identity are not the same thing.

    06
    Reputation can belong to a persistent human presence.

    When identities are harder to throw away and replace, behavior can carry history. Users remain responsible for what they build, contribute, and say under that persistent presence.

    PRIVACY BY DESIGN

    Prove you are human.
    Not who you are.

    Proof of personhood should not require turning a social network into an identity database. HumanKey is designed around data minimization: process what is necessary to establish personhood and uniqueness, then use secure credentials and privacy-preserving proofs for ordinary access.

    No public legal identity required

    Users can participate without publishing their government name or other civil identity information.

    Face verification is not ordinary login

    Passkeys are intended to handle routine authentication after successful HumanKey enrollment.

    Uniqueness, not surveillance

    The biometric system exists to establish one-person, one-account uniqueness, not to track a person's activity around the network.

    IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

    A verified human can still be wrong, dishonest, or an asshole.

    HumanKey proves personhood, not character. It cannot determine whether a statement is true. It cannot prevent humans from coordinating. It cannot eliminate harassment, fraud, misinformation, manipulation, or bad judgment. What it can do is remove one of the internet's most powerful force multipliers: the ability for one operator to cheaply masquerade as an unlimited number of independent people.

    That distinction matters. Hash Humanity is not trying to create a network where everyone agrees. It is trying to create one where disagreement is actually happening between people.

    THE MISSION

    Make the internet
    human again.

    Hash Humanity is an experiment in what social media can become when the network begins with a scarce resource software cannot manufacture: a real human being.
